Problem

You can't really know if the price is fair

Hiring a contractor is stressful, time consuming, and non transparent. The same job comes back with wildly different numbers, and homeowners are left guessing whether any of the quotes are honest. Across interviews, one line came up again and again: you can't really know if the price is fair.

Insight

The price anxiety is really scope anxiety

Every homeowner who felt unsure about price was actually unsure about scope. When five contractors propose five different interventions, the quotes describe different work, so they can't be compared. Fix the scope and the price finally becomes legible.

Solution

An AI that works like a knowledgeable friend, not a search engine

Homewise does what a trusted neighbor would do. It defines the scope first, sends one scope to every contractor, brings back quotes you can actually compare, and pauses for your approval before anything happens.

Define the scope, in plain language

You describe the problem in your own words. The agent turns it into a structured, editable scope of work, the most important artifact in the whole product.

One scope, every contractor

The same scope goes out to every contractor, so you stop repeating yourself on the phone and everyone quotes the exact same job.

A match you can trust, not a list to sort

Homewise surfaces a verified, fairly priced match with its reasoning: a match confidence, the verification badges, and how many Homewisers recommend them. Social proof, the way a neighbor refers someone, not an anonymous star rating.

Compare apples to apples, then you sign off

Bids line up side by side with a plain language summary and outlier flags, the comparison homeowners reach for ChatGPT to fake today. The agent recommends, but nothing happens until you approve. A deliberate, human moment, never an autopilot default.

Design System

Hearth, the warmth at the center of every home

Homewise runs on Hearth, a token based design system built as living code. Three semantic roles carry meaning before color does: sage for trust, ember for caution, sky for info. Shadows are banned. Separation comes from hairlines and warm contrast, and new tokens pass a drift linter before they ship.

Outcome

A working product, not a mockup

Homewise is live and interactive, a real React build with its own design system and a token linter behind it. The whole flow runs end to end, from the first plain language request to the moment you approve the hire.

Reflection

What I learned

The hardest part of an AI product isn't the model, it's trust. Every decision in Homewise, from defining scope before price to making the user approve the final hire, was about making an autonomous system feel accountable to the person it works for.
