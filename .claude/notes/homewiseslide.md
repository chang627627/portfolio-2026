The frame is pinned to `1920 / 1204` with a `.clip--play` override at `1920 / 994`, so `object-fit` has nothing to crop and nothing to letterbox. `mdls` returns null for these mp4s; read `videoWidth`/`videoHeight` off a `<video>` in the browser instead. (2) **The clip's `flex-basis` MUST be 0, not `auto`** — with `auto` the aspect-ratio's own content size (width 100% → 1680 → height 945) feeds the basis and survives shrinking, so the frame overran the slide by 18px and got clipped by `overflow:hidden`. `flex: 1 1 0` + `min-height: 0` makes the height purely leftover free space. (3) **`fit()` reserves a 160px chrome lane, not coffeeslide's 76px** — the stage is CREAM here, so the light brand/counter/nav-circle chrome must stay fully inside the ink-900 letterbox or it lands on the slide and disappears. 160 guarantees ≥80px top and bottom at every viewport (verified 1440×900 and 1024×768: zero chrome/stage overlap). **Clip posters + two-slide warming 2026-08-03 (per user "design system video can not play in the actual live site"):** the video was never broken — live serves 200/`video/mp4` with **Range 206** (Safari's requirement), the `moov` atom is at the front (faststart), and the codec is H.264/`avc1`, not HEVC. The failure was PERCEPTUAL: the five looping clips were `preload="none"` **with no `poster`**, so arriving at a slide showed a flat `--canvas-deep` rectangle until enough of a 6-24MB file arrived (hearth alone is 24MB; on a 10 Mbps line that is ~20s of empty plate, worse on a cold `x-vercel-cache: MISS`). **TWO REAL PLAYBACK BUGS were found and fixed the same day, and the posters alone would NOT have been enough:** (i) **the clips were not actually playing on arrival** — `syncMedia()` called `v.play()` while the element was still at `readyState 0` (which is exactly where `preload="none"` leaves it), that play is aborted by the element's own resource selection, and the old `.catch(() => {})` swallowed it, so the slide sat on its poster looking frozen. Replaced with a `playClip()` helper that flips `preload='auto'` FIRST, calls play, and **retries once on `canplay`** (bailing if the deck moved on). Verify with a pixel diff — draw the video to a canvas twice and compare — because `paused`/`readyState` alone read as healthy while nothing moves. (ii) **preload was flipped to 'auto' on the way past and never flipped back**, so by the late slides every clip (~119MB) was still pulling in the background and starving whatever you were trying to watch; this is what made slide 16 crawl. Now a **sliding window**: current slide + next two are `auto`, everything else is released to `none` (verified: 3 warm at slide 10, 2 at 13, 0 by 16). Fixed by (a) **frame-0 posters for all five clips** (`images/homewise-{scope,match,compare,track,hearth}-poster.jpg`, 1400px/~110-136KB each) and (b) **warming the next TWO slides** instead of one. **Poster extraction technique, since this machine has NO ffmpeg:** `qlmanage -t` picks a *representative middle* frame, which makes the poster jump when playback starts at 0 — instead compile a ~15-line Swift `AVAssetImageGenerator` (`requestedTimeToleranceBefore/After = .zero`, `appliesPreferredTrackTransform = true`) with `swiftc`, which gives exact frame 0 so poster→playback is seamless. **Re-encoding was TRIED AND REJECTED:** `avconvert` exposes no bitrate control, only resolution presets, and `Preset1280x720` turned the 24MB hearth clip into 20MB at 1280x804 — a third of the resolution for a 17% saving. Not worth it; revisit only if ffmpeg is ever installed (CRF would halve size at full resolution).
