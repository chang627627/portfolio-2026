Coffee Chat

AI-matched pairings that take the social pressure out of meeting hybrid teammates.

The bet

People will meet a stranger if you take the pressure out

Litespace sells engagement for hybrid and remote teams, and Coffee Chat was its 0-to-1 bet on the hardest part of that: getting two people who have never spoken to actually meet. Every tool on the market proved they will not do it over direct messages. So the question that decides the feature is not whether we can match people. It is whether they will say yes.

Overview

Bridging structured meetings and casual interactions

Litespace is an AI-powered enterprise platform that helps organizations improve engagement in hybrid and remote workplaces. Coffee Chat is a 0-to-1 feature designed to spark spontaneous, meaningful conversations across distributed teams.

Problem

Three things made this hard to design

01

Nobody wants to go first

Nothing tells you why you were paired, so accepting the invite and starting the conversation both carry social pressure.

02

A good pairing has to be inferred

There is no rich profile to match on. The only signals available are lightweight: profiles, onboarding inputs, and shared Slack channels.

03

Scheduling has two sides

Both people have to land on one time, and every existing tool pushes that into direct messages, where message overload and drop-off follow.

Few tools clearly communicate why a pairing was made, or reduce the social pressure of starting the conversation. That gap shaped Coffee Chat: intentional pairing with a low-pressure experience, no direct-message coordination required.

Goals and constraints

Launching ahead of the rebrand

The rebrand from a green identity to a blue one was still in progress, and we had two weeks to design and ship. That scoped the MVP to one meaningful chat per week, and anything that added friction had to go.

Meaningful pairings

Explain the match, and people accept it

AI-generated intro

A warm profile summary grounded in each person's background, so users understand who they're meeting beyond a static bio.

Explainable match

Transparent reasons for the pairing give users the confidence to accept, instead of dismissing a random match.

Powered by GPT-3

I championed using the GPT-3 API to infer connections from lightweight signals like profiles, onboarding inputs, and shared Slack channels, then generate a plain-language reason for each match.

The user flow

Remove the messaging, remove the drop-off

Existing tools rely on back-and-forth DMs that create friction and drop-offs. Coffee Chat removes messaging entirely, replacing it with default states, automated scheduling, and clear fallback paths.

Our Coffee Chat flow

  • ✓ Automated pairing and scheduling

Final Design · 1 of 5

Scheduling with contextual matchmaking

Coffee Chat auto-pairs users each week based on Google Calendar availability. Matches carry shared interests and suggested topics, and confirmation adds a Meet link to both calendars.

Final Design · 2 of 5

Handling chat declines without friction

If either person declines, both see a notification. No reason is required. Their next chat is simply scheduled for the following cycle.

Final Design · 3 of 5

Rescheduling from recommended slots

To reschedule, users pick from alternative slots automatically recommended from both people's availability. Pre-selected options keep it quick.

Final Design · 4 of 5

Reviewing the other person's proposed times

If the other person proposes new times, users pick the one that works for both. No DMs, no coordination thread.

Final Design · 5 of 5

Proposing your own availability

If none of the suggested times work, users propose their own availability for the other person to review, so scheduling never hits a dead end.

Design System

Building the atomic foundations of the rebrand

I partnered with another designer to build the atomic foundations of the new identity, defining color tokens and typography. I also created the molecule-level components on top of them: buttons, badges, alerts, and cards.

Reflection

What I learned

With only two weeks, I focused the MVP on one meaningful weekly pairing and removed anything that added friction. Working with early AI pairing logic showed me how contextual signals shape user trust.

Designing Coffee Chat mid-rebrand taught me to design for the identity that was coming, not the one being retired.
