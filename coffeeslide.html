Coffee Chat

Lead Product Designer Litespace Sep 2023

Overview

Bridging structured meetings and casual interactions

Litespace is an AI-powered enterprise platform that helps organizations improve engagement in hybrid and remote workplaces. Coffee Chat is a 0-to-1 feature designed to spark spontaneous, meaningful conversations across distributed teams.

It turns routine meetings into casual yet purposeful interactions that strengthen culture.

Problem

Remote teams lack casual social interaction

No spontaneous interaction

In hybrid and remote settings, chances for spontaneous, meaningful conversation are limited, reducing team cohesion.

A sense of disconnection

Traditional work models create distance between teammates, especially where remote and in-office work mix.

Rigid communication channels

Existing meeting structures are formal and rigid, making casual, purposeful interaction hard to foster.

Exploration · Challenge 1

Creating meaningful pairings

AI-generated intro

A warm profile summary grounded in each person's background, so users understand who they're meeting beyond a static bio.

Explainable match

Transparent reasons for the pairing give users the confidence to accept, instead of dismissing a random match.

I championed using the GPT-3 API to infer connections from lightweight signals like profiles, onboarding inputs, and shared Slack channels, then generate a plain-language reason for each match.

Exploration · Challenge 2

Mapping the user flow

Existing tools rely on back-and-forth DMs that create friction and drop-offs. Coffee Chat removes messaging entirely, replacing it with default states, automated scheduling, and clear fallback paths.

Final Design · 1 of 5

Seamless scheduling with contextual matchmaking

Coffee Chat auto-pairs users each week based on availability, integrated with Google Calendar. Matches carry shared interests and suggested topics, and the chat is set to "Going" by default. On confirmation, the system generates a Google Meet link and adds it to both calendars.

Final Design · 2 of 5

Handling chat declines without friction

If either participant declines, both users see a notification. No reason is required. Users are simply told their next coffee chat will be scheduled in the following cycle.

Final Design · 3 of 5

Flexible scheduling for better availability

To reschedule, users pick from alternative slots automatically recommended from both people's availability. Pre-selected options keep it quick. Once both agree on a new time, the chat is confirmed.

Final Design · 4 of 5

Review and select proposed times

If the other person proposes new times, users review and pick a slot that works for both. It stays flexible while keeping a structured approach, reducing the need for back-and-forth coordination.

Final Design · 5 of 5

Propose your own availability

If none of the suggested times work, users can propose their own availability for the other person to review. This flexible approach increases the likelihood of finding a time that works for both.

Design System

A new identity design system

I partnered with another designer to build the atomic foundations of the new identity, defining color tokens and typography. I also created molecule-level components that balance consistency with flexibility across product needs.

Reflection

What I learned

Designing Coffee Chat during the shift from a green identity to a blue-focused system taught me to stay flexible while keeping the experience clear and lightweight.

With only two weeks, I focused the MVP on one meaningful weekly pairing and removed anything that added friction. Working with early AI pairing logic gave me a deeper understanding of how contextual signals shape user trust.
